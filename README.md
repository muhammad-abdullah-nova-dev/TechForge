## 🧭 What is TechForge?

**TechForge** is a long-term technical knowledge repository designed to turn scattered learning into a **structured, searchable, continuously growing knowledge system**.

The repository follows a deliberate progression:

```text
Programming Fundamentals
          ↓
         OOP
          ↓
         DSA
          ↓
          OS
          ↓
   Web Development
          ↓
       Frontend
          ↓
       Backend
          ↓
         APIs
          ↓
          AI
          ↓
     Data Science
          ↓
       Interview
```

The objective is not to collect random notes.

The objective is to **build understanding progressively**.

---

# 🎯 Core Philosophy

> ### One concept. One clear explanation. One step forward.

TechForge is built around five principles:

**01 — Fundamentals First**
Understand the foundations before relying on frameworks, libraries, and abstractions.

**02 — Depth Over Memorization**
Know how and why something works, not just its definition.

**03 — Structured Learning**
Related concepts belong together and advanced topics should build on earlier knowledge.

**04 — Interview Awareness**
Important concepts should be explainable clearly under interview pressure.

**05 — Consistency Compounds**
A small amount of high-quality learning every day becomes a huge technical knowledge base over time.

# 📖 Documentation Format

TechForge documents should aim to be **clear, practical, and interview-relevant**.

A typical concept can follow this structure:

```text
# Concept Name

## 📌 What Is It?

## 🎯 Why Does It Matter?

## ⚙️ How Does It Work?

## 🧪 Example

## 🔍 Deep Dive

## ⚖️ Comparison

## ✅ Advantages

## ❌ Disadvantages

## 🌍 Real-World Usage

## 💼 Interview Answer

## ❓ Common Interview Questions

## 🧠 Follow-Up Questions

## ⚡ Quick Revision

## 🔗 Related Concepts
```

Not every document needs every section.

**Use the structure that makes the concept easiest to understand.**

# ✨ Quality Standards

A TechForge document should ideally be:

* **Accurate**
* **Readable**
* **Structured**
* **Concise where possible**
* **Deep where necessary**
* **Example-driven**
* **Interview-relevant**
* **Easy to revise later**

Avoid:

* Copy-pasting huge articles
* Extremely shallow definitions
* Unexplained jargon
* Unstructured walls of text
* Information with no practical context
